# CSM Foundations and Customer Lifecycle

Customer Success is a proactive function focused on helping customers achieve desired outcomes through a product or service. It is not simply keeping customers happy or routing support tickets.

## CSM responsibilities
Understand why the customer bought; accelerate time to first value; drive meaningful adoption; monitor risk; build multi-level relationships; demonstrate business value; protect retention; identify legitimate expansion; coordinate internally; and turn feedback into organizational learning.

## Lifecycle
Acquire → Sales-to-CS handoff → onboard → adopt → realize value → retain → expand → advocate.

At handoff capture purchase rationale, business problem, promised outcomes, stakeholders, scope, dependencies, risks and renewal date. At each stage define customer needs, CSM objective, owner, milestone and evidence.

## CSM versus Support
Support is primarily reactive: diagnose and resolve a reported issue. CSM is proactive: help the customer achieve outcomes, adopt workflows, realize value and manage risk. A CSM may coordinate support but should not become a ticket-routing function.

## Core rule
Connect every activity to the customer's business goal. Activity, sentiment and usage are useful signals; they are not substitutes for demonstrated outcomes.
