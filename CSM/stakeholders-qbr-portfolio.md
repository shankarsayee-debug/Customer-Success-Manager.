# Stakeholder Management, QBRs and Portfolio Management

## Stakeholder roles
- Economic buyer: controls or strongly influences budget.
- Champion: advocates for the solution internally.
- User: performs day-to-day workflows.
- Administrator: configures and operates the system.
- Executive sponsor: provides senior strategic support.
- Blocker: can prevent progress.
- Procurement, Legal, Security and IT: influence commercial or implementation decisions.

Map influence, priorities and engagement. Build multiple relationships, validate who owns budget/renewal/technical decisions and revisit the map after organizational changes.

## QBR/EBR agenda
1. Customer business goals and priorities.
2. Success-plan progress and KPI outcomes.
3. Adoption and workflow performance.
4. Wins and value demonstrated.
5. Challenges, support themes and risks.
6. Strategic recommendations.
7. Relevant adjacent needs/opportunities.
8. Next-quarter plan, owners and dates.

A QBR is a business review, not a feature-demo meeting. Separate measured outcomes from estimates. Finish with decisions and actions.

## Portfolio prioritization
Segment by ARR/value, complexity, strategic importance, lifecycle, health, renewal risk, growth potential and service model (high-touch, mid-touch, low-touch or tech-touch).

Prioritize using **risk × impact × urgency × opportunity**, plus likelihood that intervention changes the outcome and effort required. Critical production issues and high-value/high-risk accounts generally precede routine check-ins.

## Operating rhythm
Review health changes, renewals, overdue commitments, blockers and customer meetings weekly. Update success/account plans and reserve time for proactive adoption and value work.
